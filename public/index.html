With a will,
you decide.

Say you’re married with two young children, and everything you own comes to $500,000. Keep scrolling and watch the formula in Michigan’s intestacy law work.

Now try it with your own family. The numbers use Michigan’s amounts and count only what passes through probate.

Your estate$500,000

  1. Your spouse takes the first .MCL 700.2102(1)(b), amount

  2. Then half of what’s left: another .MCL 700.2102(1)(b)

  3. Your children split the other . Young children usually need a court-appointed conservator to hold it, and at 18 it’s theirs outright.MCL 700.2103

  4. No one is named to raise them if you both die. A judge picks.MCL 700.5202

  5. With a will, you choose: everything to your spouse, a trust for the kids until the age you pick, and the guardian you trust.

Questions

Do I need a will if I don’t own much?

If you have children, a will is the only way to name their guardian. Without one, Michigan law decides who inherits and a judge appoints someone to handle your estate.

Can my son or daughter help me fill it out?

Yes, they can read the questions with you. The answers must be yours, so if someone named in your plan helps, the attorney speaks with you privately before approving.

I own a farm, a business, or land in another state.

Add rush review at checkout and the attorney reviews your plan within one business day. If your date is very close, call us first.

Yes, for a limited purpose: once the firm accepts your matter after a conflict check, Truckey Nofsinger, LLP represents you for preparing these documents. The engagement terms you see at checkout explain exactly what’s included.
